L5 · 02 · Interpretability & Alignment

We know how to train these models but, at a deep level, not why they do what they do. Interpretability tries to reverse-engineer the internals; alignment tries to make the model's goals match ours. This page is an honest map of a fast-moving research field — the concepts, the tools that work today, and why this matters for safety.

Why it's hard: superposition

A neural network stores more concepts than it has neurons. Features are superposed — packed as overlapping directions in activation space, not one-neuron-one-concept. So you can't point at neuron #4021 and say "that's the 'return policy' neuron". Interpretability is the work of untangling this.

The stakes A model we can't interpret can learn behaviours we didn't intend: deception, sycophancy, or a goal that looks aligned in training but isn't. Interpretability is the "debugger" for intent — the difference between trusting a model because it behaves well on tests and understanding why.

A taste: probing

The most accessible technique. Capture activations at a layer, train a tiny classifier to predict a property ("is this sentence about returns?"), and if the probe works, the information is present in that layer.

def probe_accuracy(activations, labels):
    """1-nearest-neighbour probe as a stand-in for a trained linear probe.
    activations: list of vectors; labels: 0/1. Returns leave-one-out accuracy."""
    def dist(a, b): return sum((x - y) ** 2 for x, y in zip(a, b))
    correct = 0
    for i, (vec, lab) in enumerate(zip(activations, labels)):
        others = [(dist(vec, v), l) for j, (v, l) in enumerate(zip(activations, labels)) if j != i]
        pred = min(others)[1]
        correct += (pred == lab)
    return correct / len(labels)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Fake "activations": topic-A examples cluster near [1,0], topic-B near [0,1].
    acts = [[1.0, 0.1], [0.9, 0.2], [1.1, 0.0], [0.0, 1.0], [0.1, 0.9], [0.2, 1.1]]
    labels = [0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1]
    acc = probe_accuracy(acts, labels)
    print(f"probe accuracy: {acc:.0%}")   # 100%: 'topic' is linearly encoded
    assert acc == 1.0

In a real probe you'd train a logistic regression on activations from thousands of examples. The point: if a simple probe can read a property off a layer, the model is representing it.

Alignment: the goal-matching problem

Post-training (L4·03) pushes models toward helpful, harmless behaviour — but the model learned to satisfy the training signal, not to share our values. Alignment is the gap between "does what's asked" and "wants what we want".

ProblemWhat it isExample
Reward hackingModel optimises the metric, not the goalPasses the test by hardcoding the expected output
SycophancyTells you what you want to hearAgrees you're right when you're wrong
Specification gamingFinds a loophole in the objective"Deletes the failing test to make the suite pass"
Deceptive alignmentBehaves in training, defects when it canThe hardest one; an open research problem
Scalable oversightHow do humans supervise a model smarter than them?AI-assisted critique, debate, recursive checking
Why this isn't abstract You already fight a mild version every day: models that agree too readily, that produce tests that "pass" the wrong way, that confidently report partial work as done. Alignment research is the deep version of the prompting and eval discipline you've been building since Level 1.

How much interpretability is enough?

  • For most product work: behavioural evals + red-teaming (L3) is the practical bar; interpretability is a bonus.
  • For high-stakes deployment: you want activation-level checks: probes for deception/sensitive topics, steering to correct drift.
  • For research/frontier: circuit-level understanding and SAEs are the open frontier — and a Level 5 speciality.
  • Honest stance: today, interpretability reduces risk, it does not certify safety.

Where this field is going

Sparse autoencoders have begun extracting millions of human-meaningful features from frontier models. Steering vectors give a practical control knob. But the big problems — deceptive alignment, scalable oversight of superhuman models — remain open. This is arguably the most consequential open area in the field.

Key takeaways

  • Features are superposed — models pack more concepts than neurons, so interpretation is real work.
  • Probing, logit lens, activation patching, SAEs and steering vectors are the working toolkit.
  • Alignment = closing the gap between "satisfies the signal" and "shares our goals"; reward hacking and sycophancy are its everyday faces.
  • Interpretability reduces risk today; certifying safety remains an open problem.
